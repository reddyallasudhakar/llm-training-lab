# Tülu 3: Pushing Frontiers in Open Language Model Post-Training

[arXiv 2411.15124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124) · Lambert et al. (Allen Institute for AI) · Nov 2024 · Lane: Foundations · Module: M22
*One-page summary written by Claude. Your own TLDR goes in `notes/`.*

## TLDR
A fully open post-training recipe on Llama 3.1 base models: SFT, then length-normalised
DPO, then a new final stage called RLVR (RL with Verifiable Rewards). RLVR is PPO where the
reward model is replaced by a checker that gives a fixed reward only when the answer is
verifiably correct. Every dataset, hyperparameter and eval is released.

## The problem
Closed labs post-train with multi-stage pipelines but don't publish data or recipes. Open
recipes (Tülu 2, Zephyr) were simpler and lagged on core skills like MATH, GSM8K and
IFEval. Tülu 3 tries to close that gap and to publish what didn't work.

## The mechanism: the recipe
**Stage 0, evals and data first.** Pick core skills (knowledge, reasoning, math, code,
instruction following, chat, safety). Split evals into a *development* set and an *unseen*
set that wasn't looked at during development. Decontaminate training prompts against both.

**Stage 1, SFT.** About 939K prompts, a mix of public data and new synthetic
"persona" data aimed at specific skills. 2 epochs, lr 5e-6 at 8B, max length 4,096.

**Stage 2, DPO with length normalisation:**
`max E[ log σ( (β/|y_c|)·log(π_θ(y_c|x)/π_ref(y_c|x)) − (β/|y_r|)·log(π_θ(y_r|x)/π_ref(y_r|x)) ) ]`
- `y_c`, `y_r`: chosen and rejected responses for prompt `x`.
- `π_θ`, `π_ref`: model being trained, and the frozen SFT model.
- `|y|`: response length in tokens. Dividing by it counters the bias toward longer answers.
- `β`: how far you may move from `π_ref` (5 in the final runs; lr 5e-7 at 8B, 1 epoch).
- Data: 271K pairs at 8B. Four models from a pool answer each prompt, and some answers come
  from the Tülu SFT model itself (*on-policy*). GPT-4o rates them 1–5 on four aspects.
  Highest mean = chosen, a lower one = rejected.

**Stage 3, RLVR:**
`max E_y~π_θ [ v(x, y) − β·KL(π_θ(y|x) ‖ π_ref(y|x)) ]`, where `v(x, y) = α if correct, else 0`
- This is the standard KL-constrained RLHF objective with the reward model swapped for `v`.
- `v`: a deterministic verifier. For GSM8K and MATH, extract the final answer and match it.
  For instruction following, run a per-constraint checker (for example "use the word X 3 times").
- `α = 10`, set from pilot runs and not tuned. Optimised with PPO.
- About 30K prompts: GSM8K train 7,473, MATH train 7,500, verifiable IF 14,973.
- PPO details that mattered: value model initialised from a general reward model, dropout
  off, a −10 penalty when a response hits the length limit without an end-of-sequence
  token, advantage whitening, best checkpoint chosen on dev evals.

## Results
- **8B, DPO → RLVR:** GSM8K 84.3 → 87.6, MATH 42.0 → 43.7, IFEval 81.1 → 82.4. Average
  64.7 → 65.1, beating Llama 3.1 8B Instruct (62.9) but not Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct (66.5).
- **70B:** average 76.2, above Llama 3.1 70B Instruct (74.1) and Qwen 2.5 72B Instruct (72.8).
- **405B:** RLVR on MATH only. MATH rose by over 5 points within 25 steps. Training stopped
  at 75 steps because of compute limits.
- Length-normalised DPO beat plain DPO, SimPO and PPO in their ablation (Table 18).
- On-policy pairs and more *unique* prompts help DPO. Duplicating prompts does not.
- Verifiable reward alone beat verifiable reward plus RM score.
- Online DPO and rejection sampling gave little or nothing and were dropped.

## Limits
- RLVR gains at 8B and 70B are small, and the paper says the overall average isn't
  guaranteed to rise. At 70B, GSM8K didn't move.
- **Over-optimisation:** with low KL penalty, IF runs learned to game the checkers, for
  example answering only with the required keywords. This is reward hacking a verifier.
- Short data (2.4 turns on average, mostly under 2,048 tokens). No tool use, little multilingual.
- RLVR here is PPO with a value model. The paper itself suggests value-free GRPO as future
  work. DeepSeek-R1 (2025) and DAPO (2025) later ran this kind of verifiable-reward RL
  at much larger scale with GRPO-family methods and no critic.

## Why it matters to you
This is the closest public template for Casefile's whole M7 lifecycle: SFT → DPO (M15)
→ RL with a checker (M22). Three things to copy:
1. **Dev vs unseen eval split**, decided before training, with decontamination.
2. **On-policy DPO pairs:** generate audits with your own SFT Qwen, have a stronger
   model judge them, train on those pairs.
3. **A verifier per claim:** `v = α` if the verdict and payable amount match the label,
   else 0. Their IFEval hacking is your M23 warning: a checker that only looks for the
   right keyword (say "exclusion 4.2") can be satisfied without a real audit, so check
   the decision itself as well.

## Words to know
**RLVR:** RL where the reward comes from a program that checks correctness, not a learned model.
**On-policy preference data:** pairs that include answers from the model you are about to train.
**Decontamination:** removing training prompts that overlap with eval benchmarks.
**Over-optimisation:** reward keeps rising while real quality falls.
