# DSPy: Compiling Declarative Language Model Calls into Self-Improving Pipelines

[arXiv 2310.03714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03714) · Khattab et al. (Stanford, UC Berkeley, and others) · Oct 2023 · Lane: Foundations · Module: M25
*One-page summary written by Claude. Your own TLDR goes in `notes/`.*

## TLDR
Stop hand-writing prompt strings. Declare what each LLM call takes in and returns, wire the
calls together in Python, and let a "compiler" pick few-shot examples (or fine-tune a small
model) to maximise a metric you define. It is PyTorch's idea applied to prompts: modules
plus an optimiser instead of hand-tuned weights.

## The problem
LLM pipelines were built from long prompt templates found by trial and error. A template that
works for one model, task or pipeline often breaks on another. The authors compare this to
hand-tuning the weights of a classifier.

## The mechanism
Three abstractions:
- **Signature:** a typed declaration of a text transformation, e.g. `"question -> answer"` or
  `"context, question -> search_query"`. It says *what*, not *how* to prompt.
- **Module:** a layer that implements a signature. `Predict` formats the prompt, calls the
  LM and parses fields. `ChainOfThought` adds a rationale field first. `ReAct`,
  `MultiChainComparison` and `Retrieve` also exist. A **program** is a Python class whose
  `forward()` calls modules with ordinary loops and ifs.
- **Teleprompter (optimiser):** takes a program, a small training set and a metric, and
  returns an optimised program.

Each module's learnable parameters are: which LM to call, its instructions and field
prefixes, and (the focus of this paper) its **demonstrations**. Compiling then solves:

`best_demos = argmax over demo sets D of  (1/|V|) Σ_(x,y) in V  metric(program_D(x), y)`

- **D** = the few-shot examples placed in each module's prompt. **V** = a validation set.
- **metric** = any Python function: exact match, F1, or another DSPy program.

How **BootstrapFewShot** builds candidate demos:
1. Run a teacher program (by default, the zero-shot version of your program) on training inputs.
2. Record the input and output of *every* module along the way (the trace).
3. Keep traces where the final output passes the metric. Their intermediate steps become
   demos, so you only need labels for the final output, not for each step.
4. **Random search** (or Optuna) tries different demo sets and keeps the best on V.

Extras: compile again with the compiled program as teacher (`bootstrap×2`); **ensemble** the
top candidates with majority vote; **BootstrapFinetune** turns the kept traces into training
data and fine-tunes a small model, such as T5-Large, for each step.

## Results
Only 200 training examples and no hand-written prompts.
- **GSM8K** (math): compiling raised GPT-3.5 from 33% to 82% and Llama2-13b-chat from 9% to
  47%. GPT-3.5 CoT with bootstrapped demos matched CoT with human reasoning chains (dev 80.3 vs 78.6).
- **HotPotQA** (multi-hop QA): compiled programs went from 32% to 46% answer EM with GPT-3.5,
  and from 22% to 41% with Llama2-13b-chat. A simple 2-hop retrieval program did best. Compiled Llama2-13b-chat became
  competitive with GPT-3.5.
- **Fine-tuning:** a T5-Large (770M) multihop program reached 39.3% EM and 46.0% passage
  accuracy on dev, from 200 labelled and 800 unlabelled questions.
- Compiling takes minutes to tens of minutes: a few thousand program runs.

## Limits
- Two tasks, two main LMs (GPT-3.5, Llama2-13b-chat), and most comparisons are on dev sets.
- The paper optimises demonstrations, not instructions. Long demo lists also make prompts expensive.
- **Later work:** MIPROv2 (Opsahl-Ong et al., 2024) added joint instruction and demo search
  with Bayesian optimisation. **GEPA** (2507.19457) then found that reflective,
  instruction-only optimisation beat MIPROv2, with prompts up to 9.2× shorter.
- The metric is the whole training signal. A weak metric gets you a program that games it.

## Why it matters to you
At M25 this is the frame for Casefile's prompt-optimisation comparison (sprints 18–19).
1. **Write T3 as a DSPy program:** `"policy, claim_facts, insurer_decision -> verdict, rule, payable_amount"`,
   with your rules engine as the metric: verdict right, amount within ±₹1, rule cited.
2. **The baseline your fine-tuning must beat.** Compile it for the same Qwen size you
   fine-tune. If compiled prompting gets close to SFT, that changes the story of your results table.
3. **BootstrapFinetune is distillation:** keeping a strong model's metric-passing traces to
   train a small one is close to your D1 stage. Compare the two pipelines.

## Words to know
**Signature:** a declaration of a module's input and output fields.
**Teleprompter / optimiser:** the algorithm that tunes a program's prompts or weights against a metric.
**Bootstrapping demos:** generating examples by running the program and keeping successful traces.
**Compound AI system:** a program that chains several LLM calls and tools.
