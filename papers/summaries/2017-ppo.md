# Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms

[arXiv 1707.06347](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347) · Schulman, Wolski, Dhariwal, Radford, Klimov · Jul 2017 · Lane: Foundations · Module: M20
*One-page summary written by Claude. Your own TLDR goes in `notes/`.*

## TLDR
A policy-gradient method that lets you take several optimisation passes over each batch
of experience without the policy blowing up. It does this by clipping how far the
probability of each action can move. It gets most of TRPO's stability with a few lines
of code.

## The problem
Vanilla policy gradient uses each sample once. If you reuse a batch for many gradient
steps, updates get destructively large. TRPO fixes this with a hard KL constraint, but
it needs second-order optimisation and doesn't fit setups like a shared policy/value network.

## The mechanism
**The probability ratio:** `r_t(θ) = π_θ(a_t | s_t) / π_θ_old(a_t | s_t)`
- `π_θ`: the policy you're updating. `π_θ_old`: the policy that collected the batch.
- `a_t`, `s_t`: the action and state at step t. For an LLM: the token, and the prompt
  plus tokens so far.
- `r_t = 1` means unchanged. `r_t = 1.5` means the action is now 50% more likely.

**The advantage `Â_t`:** how much better this action was than expected. Positive: do
more of it. Negative: do less. PPO estimates it with GAE (Eq. 11–12):
`δ_t = r_t + γ·V(s_{t+1}) − V(s_t)`, then `Â_t = δ_t + (γλ)·δ_{t+1} + …`.
(Here `r_t` is the reward, not the ratio.) `V` is a learned value function, `γ` the
discount, `λ` trades bias against variance.

**The clipped surrogate objective** (Eq. 7):
`L_CLIP(θ) = E_t[ min( r_t(θ)·Â_t ,  clip(r_t(θ), 1−ε, 1+ε)·Â_t ) ]`, with ε = 0.2.
- The first term is the plain surrogate TRPO maximises.
- The clipped term removes any reward for pushing `r_t` outside [1−ε, 1+ε].
- Taking the `min` makes this a pessimistic lower bound. You ignore a ratio change when
  it would make the objective look *better*, and keep it when it makes things *worse*.
- **What the clip prevents:** if `Â > 0`, once the action is 20% more likely the gradient
  is zero, so you can't over-exploit one lucky sample. If `Â < 0`, once it's 20% less
  likely you stop pushing it down. Mistakes are never clipped away. Over K epochs on the
  same batch, the policy can't run far from the one that collected the data.

**The full loss** (Eq. 9): `L_CLIP − c1·(V_θ(s_t) − V_target)² + c2·Entropy`. A
value-function error term (for a shared network) and an entropy bonus for exploration.

**Algorithm:** N actors each run `π_old` for T steps; compute advantages; optimise L for
K epochs with minibatches of size M ≤ NT; set `θ_old ← θ`; repeat.

**Alternative tested:** a KL penalty `β·KL(π_old, π_θ)` where β halves when KL falls below
target/1.5 and doubles when it exceeds target×1.5. It did worse than clipping.

## Results
- **Objective ablation** (7 MuJoCo tasks, 3 seeds, normalised score): no clipping −0.39,
  clip ε = 0.1: 0.76, **ε = 0.2: 0.82**, ε = 0.3: 0.70, best adaptive KL 0.74, best fixed KL 0.72.
- **Continuous control:** beat TRPO, CEM, A2C and vanilla PG on almost all environments.
- **Atari (49 games):** most games won on reward averaged over training (PPO 30, ACER 18,
  A2C 1). On final performance ACER won more (28 vs 19).
- MuJoCo settings: horizon 2048, Adam 3e-4, 10 epochs, minibatch 64, γ 0.99, λ 0.95.

## Limits
- Benchmarks are robotics and games, not language. Applying it to LLMs came later.
- **The clip isn't the whole story.** *Implementation Matters in Deep Policy Gradients*
  (Engstrom et al., 2020) found much of PPO's edge over TRPO came from code-level tricks,
  and clipping doesn't really keep the policy in a trust region.
- **The critic is costly for LLMs.** *GRPO* (Shao et al., 2024, DeepSeekMath) drops the
  value network and uses the mean reward of a group of samples as the baseline. *RLOO*
  (Ahmadian et al., 2024) argued PPO's machinery is largely unnecessary for RLHF.
- **Symmetric ε can collapse entropy** in LLM RL. *DAPO* (Yu et al., 2025) raised the
  upper clip bound ("clip-higher") to keep exploration alive.

## Why it matters to you
At M20 you'll implement `L_CLIP` by hand. At M21 it becomes InstructGPT-style RLHF with a
KL penalty to the SFT model. At M22 GRPO keeps this exact ratio-and-clip and swaps GAE
for group-relative advantages. Casefile decision: classic PPO needs a policy, a
reference, a reward model and a value model in memory at once. For a small Qwen this is
why you'll likely skip PPO for GRPO on GCP. Learn PPO anyway: every GRPO knob (ε,
epochs per batch, KL to reference) comes from here.

## Words to know
**Policy:** the model's distribution over actions (for LLMs, next tokens).
**Advantage:** reward relative to what the value function expected.
**Surrogate objective:** a stand-in you can optimise that tracks true performance near `θ_old`.
**Trust region:** a limit on how far one update may move the policy.
