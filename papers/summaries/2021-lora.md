# LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models

[arXiv 2106.09685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685) · Hu, Shen, Wallis, Allen-Zhu, Li, Wang, Wang, Chen · Jun 2021 · Lane: Foundations · Module: M10
*One-page summary written by Claude. Your own TLDR goes in `notes/`.*

## TLDR
Freeze the pretrained weights and learn the weight *change* as the product of two thin
matrices. On GPT-3 175B this cut trainable parameters by 10,000× and training memory by
3×, matched or beat full fine-tuning, and adds no inference latency once merged.

## The problem
Full fine-tuning updates every weight, so each task needs its own full copy of the model.
For GPT-3 that's 175B parameters per task. Older cheap methods had costs: adapter layers
add extra sequential layers, so they slow inference (up to 30% slower at batch size 1 in
their GPT-2 test). Prefix tuning is hard to optimise and uses up part of the context window.

## The mechanism
Take any weight matrix W₀ of shape d × k. Instead of learning a full update ΔW, write
`ΔW = B A`, with `B` of shape d × r and `A` of shape r × k, and r much smaller than d and k.
The forward pass becomes (eq. 3):
`h = W₀ x + (α / r) · B A x`
- **W₀:** the pretrained weight. Frozen: no gradients, no optimizer state.
- **x / h:** the layer's input and output vectors.
- **A, B:** the only trainable parts. A starts random Gaussian; B starts at zero, so
  BA = 0 and the model behaves exactly like the base model at step 0.
- **r (rank):** how many directions the update can use. The paper uses 1 to 64.
- **α:** a constant scale. They set α to the first r they try and don't tune it; dividing
  by r means you don't have to retune the learning rate when you change r.

Why it saves memory: a full update has d·k numbers, LoRA has r·(d + k). For a
4096 × 4096 matrix at r = 8 that's about 65k trainable numbers instead of 16.8M. With Adam
you also skip the two optimizer-state copies for every frozen weight.

**Deploying:** compute `W = W₀ + BA` once and serve it like a normal model, so there's no
extra latency. To switch tasks, subtract BA and add a different B′A′.

**Where to put it:** the paper adapts only attention matrices (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o) and
leaves the MLP frozen. With a fixed budget of 18M parameters on GPT-3, adapting W_q and W_v
at r = 4 beat putting everything into one matrix at r = 8.

**Why low rank works:** the authors' hypothesis is that the change needed to adapt a
model has low "intrinsic rank". The update ΔW boosts directions already in W₀ that
pretraining didn't emphasise (by about 21× at r = 4 in one GPT-3 layer).

## Results
- **GPT-3 175B:** LoRA with 4.7M parameters got 73.4 on WikiSQL, 91.7 on MNLI-m and
  53.8/29.8/45.9 ROUGE on SAMSum. Full fine-tuning (175,255.8M parameters): 73.8, 89.5,
  52.0/28.0/44.5. Adapters and prefix tuning did worse.
- **Training memory:** 1.2 TB down to 350 GB; checkpoint 350 GB down to 35 MB (r = 4, W_q
  and W_v only); 25% faster training.
- **Rank:** r = 1 was already competitive for W_q + W_v on WikiSQL and MNLI. Going to
  r = 64 didn't help.
- Matched or beat full fine-tuning on RoBERTa, DeBERTa and GPT-2 as well.

## Limits
- Tested on classification, NL-to-SQL and short generation, not on long instruction
  tuning or reasoning. The authors note a small r won't fit every task, e.g. a new language.
- Batching different tasks' adapters in one forward pass is awkward once weights are merged.
- **"Attention only" turned out to be weak advice.** QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023) found
  you need LoRA on all linear layers, MLP included, to match full fine-tuning; it also put
  the frozen base in 4-bit. Biderman et al. (2024, "LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less")
  found LoRA trails full fine-tuning on code and maths, but forgets less of the base
  model's skills. rsLoRA (Kalajdzievski, 2023) argued for scaling by α/√r, not α/r, so
  higher ranks actually help.

## Why it matters to you
At M10 you'll write a LoRA layer by hand, then use PEFT; M11 adds 4-bit (QLoRA). For
Casefile, the decisions are: which modules to target (start with all linear layers, per
QLoRA, not just W_q and W_v), which rank (sweep a small set like 8, 16, 32 instead of
assuming bigger is better), and whether to merge. Keep one Qwen base and save the SFT,
distillation and DPO stages as separate adapters you can compare. Then merge the winner
into the base before quantising it to run on the 16 GB M3.

## Words to know
**Rank:** the number of independent directions a matrix can represent.
**Adapter:** a small trainable add-on to a frozen model; LoRA is one kind.
**Merging:** folding BA into W₀ so inference is a plain forward pass.
**PEFT:** parameter-efficient fine-tuning, the family of methods LoRA belongs to.
