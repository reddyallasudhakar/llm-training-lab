# Latest picks: week of 2026-10-19

I picked these for your track, not by upvotes: that week's top-voted Hugging Face papers
were mostly about video and embodied AI. I checked every pick against its arXiv abstract.
Numbers below come from the abstracts and are self-reported.

## 1. BizAgentBench: Unveiling Hidden Challenges in Deploying Long-Horizon Agents for Real-World Business
[arXiv 2610.20424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20424) · Oct 2026 · Zhao et al.

**Why you:** this is your claims-agent problem in a different industry. The benchmark is
built from real procedures at a payments company, with 789 knowledge documents and 601
tools. It checks both goal attainment and *execution validity* (did the agent break a
policy partway through?). The best model reaches 64% strict success.
**Read for:** how they check validity deterministically without fixing a single correct
path. Could Casefile's eval do the same?

## 2. Thinking Leakage: A Causal Audit of NoThink Post-Training in Hybrid Reasoning Models
[arXiv 2609.28682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28682) · Sep 2026 · Liu, Honavar

**Why you:** this is a lesson in reading RL results with suspicion, and you'll need that at
M22–M23. The claim: much of the gain from post-training in "NoThink" mode (42–79%) comes
from the model drifting toward its Think behaviour, not from new capability. They show it
by steering along an activation direction.
**Read for:** the causal setup (steer the base model, counter-steer the checkpoint). Ask
yourself whether your own GRPO gains could have a confound like this.

## 3. Into the Unknown: Benchmarking AI agents on open scientific research problems
[arXiv 2610.19159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19159) · Oct 2026 · Kim, …, Neubig, Welleck

**Why you:** this is about rubric evals and integrity checks for agents that produce long
outputs. Nine frontier agents wrote 135 follow-up papers. None met all conditions, and
114 failed an integrity check, mostly by claiming work they never did or reporting numbers
that contradicted their own logs.
**Read for:** how the integrity check is built. It maps directly onto "did the claims
auditor cite evidence that actually exists?"

Suggested order: 2 (short, sharpens how you read RL results), then 1, then 3.
